My Refrigerator Was 25 Years Old Heres What I Did

James Patterson — 2026-05-18
Advertisement

I had a refrigerator in my basement for about ten years. It was a hand‑me‑down from my parents, a Harvest Gold beast from the late 1990s. It kept beer cold and frozen pizzas solid. I thought it was fine. Then one day I put a power meter on it. The thing was using 1,800 kilowatt‑hours a year. That’s about $270 at my electric rate of $0.15 per kWh. A new Energy Star fridge uses about 400 kWh per year, or $60. That old relic was costing me $210 more every single year. I had been paying that extra for ten years. That’s over $2,000. I could have bought a new fridge three times over.

I unplugged it that afternoon. I moved the beer to the kitchen fridge. I gave away the frozen pizzas. My electric bill dropped $18 the next month. I felt stupid for waiting so long.

That old fridge is not unique. Millions of homes have second refrigerators in the basement or garage. Most of them are old. Some are from the 1980s. A fridge from the 1980s uses about 2,500 kWh per year. That’s $375 at $0.15 per kWh. A new fridge uses $60. The savings from replacing that old second fridge is over $300 per year. Payback on a new $600 fridge is two years. After that, it’s pure profit.

I’m not saying you should run out and buy a new fridge tomorrow. But if you have an old one that’s just holding a few sodas and some leftovers, do the math. A power meter costs $20. Plug it in for a week. Multiply the weekly kWh by 52. Multiply by your electric rate. That’s your annual cost. If it’s over $150, it’s probably worth replacing.

I had a client named Linda who had a 1985 fridge in her garage. She used it for drinks and frozen vegetables. She didn’t think it was a big deal. I loaned her my power meter. The fridge used 2,200 kWh per year. She almost fell over. She bought a new Energy Star fridge for $550. Her electric bill dropped $26 a month. The new fridge paid for itself in 21 months. She said she wished she had done it years earlier.

The technology has changed a lot. Old fridges use a lot of energy because their compressors are inefficient, their insulation is thin, and their door seals leak. New fridges have variable speed compressors, better insulation, and tighter seals. They also use LED lighting instead of incandescent bulbs. Every part is better.

But there’s a catch. Not all new fridges are created equal. The most efficient models are those with the Energy Star Most Efficient label. They use about 20% less than standard Energy Star models. They cost more upfront, but the payback is still good. For a garage fridge that runs year‑round, it’s worth getting the best efficiency you can afford.

Another option is to get rid of the second fridge entirely. Do you really need it? I thought I did. I didn’t. The beer fit in the kitchen fridge. The frozen pizza was a convenience. I didn’t miss it. Linda kept her new fridge because she has a large family and needs the extra space. That’s fine. But at least she’s not using an antique.
